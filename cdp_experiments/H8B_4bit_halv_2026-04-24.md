## Purpose

Isolate the **halving knob** from the **counter-widening knob**. H8 changed
both (4-bit → 6-bit *and* added halving); its −0.065 pp geomean regression
vs H7 couldn't be attributed. H8B keeps H7's 4-bit saturation point but
applies the same halving mechanics, so:

|              | no halving | halving |
|--------------|:----------:|:-------:|
| **4-bit**    | H7         | **H8B** |
| **6-bit**    | — (untested) | H8 |

If H8B regresses, the halving is the root cause. If H8B matches H7, the
regression was about widening.

## Interpretation

Two surprises worth unpacking:

### 1. 4-bit halving on health is *less* bad than 6-bit halving

My pre-experiment prediction: at 4-bit, the `+3` useless amplification is
a larger fraction of the halved headroom (uf:7, us:7→10 at 4-bit vs
uf:31, us:31→34 at 6-bit), so the gate should trip *more* often and be
*worse* on health. Empirically: H8B is 1,037 cycles *better* than H8.

Hypothesis: **absolute counter range matters more than relative
arithmetic.** After halving, decay's `−1/tick` recovers the counter to
sub-gate range faster when the counter lives in a smaller absolute range.

- At 4-bit: post-halving (uf, us) sit around 7. Decay reduces them to
  sub-gate range in ~4-7 ticks.
- At 6-bit: post-halving (uf, us) sit around 31. Decay takes ~31 ticks
  to fully recover. In the meantime, more useless events arrive, re-
  tripping the gate before the PC re-opens.

So 6-bit keeps formerly-good PCs suppressed *for longer wall-clock*
once halving triggers, even though halving triggers less often. Net
result: 6-bit is worse despite fewer halving events.

### 2. H8B introduces a +62 cycle delta on treeadd

H7 and H8 are cycle-identical on treeadd (558,042). H8B is +62 cycles.
This is tiny (0.011%), but it's a new differentiation: the 4-bit
halving now flips *some* `shouldIssue` outcomes on treeadd that neither
no-halving (H7) nor 6-bit-halving (H8) touched.

Why? Treeadd has 169 halving events at 4-bit but only 30 at 6-bit. So
halving fires 5.6× more often. At 4-bit, many of treeadd's hot PCs do
reach saturation (15) regularly and halve. At 6-bit, they reach 30 or
40 but never saturate, so halving never triggers.

The +62 cycle effect on treeadd is small enough that the halving isn't
catastrophically mis-classifying PCs here — it's just nudging a few
borderline decisions.

## Takeaway

Halving-on-saturation is a **net regression across the board**, regardless
of the counter width it's paired with. The mechanism is that the `+3`
useless amplification, applied *immediately after* a halving step,
breaks the ratio-preservation invariant the halving was supposed to
protect.

**Kill the halving.** The 6-bit widening is harmless and can stay as
safety headroom, but the halving should be removed in both AccUseful
and AccUseless arms of `pcTableResp`. That brings H8/H8B back to H7's
−1.916%.
